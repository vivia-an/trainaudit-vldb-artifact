\subsection{Efficiency and Overhead}
\label{subsec:efficiency}

\paragraph{Setup.} We measure on a 1.2B-parameter GPT (24 layers, hidden 2048, 16 heads, sequence 1024) trained with DP=2 on NVIDIA H20 GPUs in bf16. The uninstrumented baseline runs at \textbf{732\,ms/step} and \textbf{81\,TFLOP/s/GPU} ($\approx$55\% MFU), a representative production step. The trace store is a single append-only table \texttt{coredump(step, stage, data\,JSON)}; a full dump writes \textbf{524 parameter rows across 25 lifecycle stages = 13{,}106 rows/step}.

\paragraph{Naive full collection is infeasible.} Checksumming and profiling every parameter at every stage, every step, costs \textbf{192\,s/step}---a \textbf{262$\times$} slowdown (Table~\ref{tab:overhead}). This is not a tuning artifact but a structural fact, and it motivates two orthogonal levers: a cheaper per-dump implementation, and periodic rather than per-step dumping.

\paragraph{Where the cost goes.} Decomposing one dump (microbenchmarks plus trace-store inspection), the dominant cost is the per-parameter SHA-256 checksum, computed on the host after a \emph{synchronous} GPU$\to$CPU copy of each tensor: \textbf{$\approx$73\% (140\,s)}. Per-parameter distribution statistics (quantiles, skewness/kurtosis, histogram) account for \textbf{$\approx$18\% (35\,s)} and DuckDB row inserts for \textbf{$\approx$2\% (4\,s)}. The synchronous per-parameter device-to-host transfer, not the hashing arithmetic, is the bottleneck.

\paragraph{Equivalent-fast collection.} We replace the two hot primitives with semantics-preserving, GPU-native implementations, leaving the trace schema and every recorded field unchanged: (i) the SHA-256 checksum becomes a deterministic integer \emph{fingerprint} computed on-device---a position-weighted modular reduction over the tensor's byte view---transferring only a scalar per parameter; (ii) the distribution statistics are computed from on-device power-sum moments and a single batched quantile call, again transferring only scalars. Both preserve detection semantics: the fingerprint is \emph{deterministic} (identical bytes yield identical fingerprints) and \emph{discriminative} (a single-element change alters it), and the recomputed moments match the reference implementation to $<10^{-7}$. The optimized dump costs \textbf{25\,s/step---a 7.7$\times$ reduction} (Table~\ref{tab:overhead}); the fingerprint alone contributes 7$\times$ and the fused statistics the remainder.

\paragraph{Detection is unaffected.} We re-ran the full collector with the fast path and compared, stage by stage, the cross-rank equality verdict that underlies the replication checks against the SHA-256 collector. The per-stage agreement rates are \emph{identical} (overall 59.9\% vs.\ 59.9\%): 100\% at synchronized-parameter stages, 0\% at per-rank gradient/optimizer-state stages, 12.5\% at the post-optimizer stage, and so on. The optimization changes the implementation, not what \textsc{TrainAudit} detects.

\paragraph{Periodic dumping makes it online.} A semantic check need not run every step. Amortizing one dump over $K$ steps gives a per-step overhead of $(T_{\text{dump}} + T_{\text{base}}(K{-}1))/(K\,T_{\text{base}}) - 1$. With the optimized collector, step overhead falls below \textbf{10\% at $K\!\approx\!380$} and below 5\% at $K\!\approx\!760$ (Table~\ref{tab:amortize})---an order of magnitude more frequent than the naive collector at the same budget.

\paragraph{Online query cost and limitations.} The online checks compile to SQL over the trace table at 2--3\,ms per rule (\S\ref{subsec:data-collector}); combined with periodic optimized collection, \textsc{TrainAudit} runs inline at single-digit-percent step overhead. The residual 25\,s/dump is dominated by the management of the 13{,}106 per-parameter items (Python dispatch, per-fingerprint scalar synchronization, and row insertion). We explored two ways to reduce it---a background writer thread (asynchronous device-to-host transfer and insertion) and per-step batched collection (a single device-to-host transfer plus a bulk insert)---but measured both to \emph{regress} in steady state: the cost of managing per-parameter GPU scalars (their lifetime, queue back-pressure, and allocator pressure) exceeds the synchronization it removes. Per-step full collection therefore has a floor near 25\,s on this configuration, and low online overhead comes from \emph{periodic} dumping (Table~\ref{tab:amortize}), not from making each individual dump cheaper.
