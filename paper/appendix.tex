$\pi_{\text{topo}}$ and $\pi_{\text{precond}}$---the distinction already stated
in the main text, not a new claim.

\section{Bug Pool Construction and Methodology}
\label{app:methodology}

This appendix documents the source, filtering, classification, and
inter-rater validation of the 392-record silent-error evidence corpus. The
merged manifest, deterministic mapping tables, disagreement sets, provenance
audit, and the available reproduction artifacts are released with the paper.

\subsection{Pool Construction}
\label{app:method-pool}

Four frameworks with auditable training-critical history:
Megatron-LM, DeepSpeed, OLMo, and OLMo-core. The corpus frozen on 2026-05-10
merges a 295-record commit-oriented pool and a 128-record issue/PR pool
(32 overlaps): retain 96 unique issue/PR records + all 295 + one fully
specified orphan $\Rightarrow$ \textbf{392 records} (Megatron-LM 110,
DeepSpeed 123, OLMo 77, OLMo-core 82). Commit diffs are the preferred
experimental unit (concrete buggy/fixed transition); the 96 issue/PR-only
records stay for catalog coverage but never enter Real-SE without a later
commit-pair audit. Screening keeps training-correctness semantic failures
that can proceed without an immediate exception and that expose extractable
objects/relation/witness; duplicate upstream incidents are collapsed.
Provenance: 289 fixed-commit IDs (288 resolve locally; dates 2021-08-06--2026-05-06),
96 URL-only, 7 summary-only. This is \emph{not} an exhaustive default-branch
census; full audit in \texttt{benchmark/eval/corpus\_provenance\_audit.md}.

\subsection{Inter-Rater Reliability}
\label{app:method-irr}

Coding consistency cannot rest on a single metric. We adopt a dual-protocol cross validation: protocol~A measures re-annotation agreement using 32 doubly-and-independently annotated records in the pool; protocol~B introduces an LLM rerater fully independent of the original annotators and computes Cohen's $\kappa$ on a 50-record stratified sample.

\paragraph{Protocol A: Re-annotation agreement.}
32 records in the pool went through two mutually independent annotation flows, forming a natural inter-rater test set. We observe \textbf{25/32 = 78.1\% raw agreement}; all 7 disagreements are released with per-record rationale, and the disagreements concentrate between the broad \textit{control\_flow} label and more specific subsystem labels (\textit{lr\_schedule}, \textit{gradient\_sync}).

\paragraph{Protocol B: Independent LLM rerater.}
We drew a 50-record sample stratified over the reporting labels, hid the
reference labels, and had an independent LLM rerater assign labels based only
on the issue title, \texttt{root\_cause}, and fix diff. Raw agreement is
60.0\% (30/50); under the chance baseline of
$p_{\text{expected}}=0.078$ for the observed label vocabulary, this gives
\textbf{Cohen's $\kappa=0.566$}. This moderate agreement reinforces why the
labels are used only for coarse corpus description rather than as the
detector's semantic representation. \texttt{irr\_50\_results.json} releases
the stripped input, independent and reference labels, per-label metrics, and
all disagreement details. Table~\ref{tab:irr} summarizes the two protocols.

\paragraph{Literature benchmark.}
Protocol~B is a single-round, no-discussion independent assignment, so its
$\kappa{=}0.566$ should be compared to \emph{first-round} agreement in
published multi-class bug-coding studies, not to final post-adjudication
values. First-round $\kappa$ is often low before discussion
(e.g., ${\sim}0.35$ for DL-framework bug labeling~\cite{chen2023understanding}),
while final agreed values after arbitration commonly exceed $0.74$
(e.g., $0.74$--$0.82$ for mobile DL deployment-fault coding~\cite{chen2021deployment}).
Under that protocol alignment, $0.566$ sits above typical first-round
baselines and remains only moderate---consistent with using these labels
solely for coarse corpus description.

\subsection{From the Evidence Corpus to Real-SE}
\label{app:method-reproducibility}

Real-SE is not a random sample of
\texttt{reproduction\_status}. Diversity targets (labels$\times$frameworks)
plus six admission criteria: (i)~auditable provenance; (ii)~silent semantic
failure; (iii)~buggy/fixed commit pair; (iv)~runnable driver; (v)~archived
commands/env/logs/verdict; (vi)~no pattern-only surrogate. Criteria and
manifest are frozen before detector scoring. Result: 17 detector-coverable
\emph{native-commit method-level} replays (every label; not end-to-end jobs)
+ boundary O-003 (deliberate miss; in buggy denominator). O-022 stays out (no
commit pair) but remains survey/catalog evidence. Detection rate is not a
prevalence estimate. Manifest: \texttt{benchmark/eval/real\_sdc/}; rebuild
\texttt{build\_392\_catalog.py}/\texttt{build\_manifest\_v2.py} (392/392;
7/32 disagreements; $\kappa{=}0.566$).

\section{Invariant Miner: Complete Algorithm Specification}
\label{app:algorithm}
\label{app:case_study}

The main-text running example is SwitchMLP \texttt{router.weight}
(three-predicate SQL). Below: verification funnel counts, one CE I/O record,
and the provenance audit. Offline mining uses five logical roles
($S_1$--$S_5$: gap analysis, evidence, synthesis, persistence, reporting;
prompt dumps with the artifact)---an engineering convenience, not the
contribution (main text).

\subsection{Four-Stage Verification Funnel}

A candidate is accepted only if it clears the main-text accept rule. Equivalently,
it is rejected when any gate fails: (1)~semantic duplicate of an existing rule;
(2)~fewer than two independent evidence sources; (3)~any constructed
counterexample evaluates to \texttt{HOLDS} on a correct execution;
(4)~$\textsc{Conf}(c) < \theta_{\text{conf}}{=}0.8$ after the hard gates pass.
Stages~1--4 of Figure~\ref{fig:mining-funnel} implement these gates in order.

\paragraph{Per-stage candidate counts and skip-stress validation.}
Figure~\ref{fig:mining-funnel} gives the per-stage counts across four target
frameworks. The 420 pattern hypotheses at Stage~1 expand to 5{,}334 concrete
candidates at Stage~2; clean-trace validation retains 3{,}436 at Stage~3;
adversarial verification retains 357 at Stage~4; and a separate manual
cross-framework curation selects 45 deployment rules. Without Stage~3,
114/400 sampled candidate--trace evaluations fire on held-out clean traces
(28.5\%). Skipping Stage~4 and evaluating Stage~3 survivors on the same
held-out protocol yields 0/6{,}922 alerts; full-funnel (Stage~4) survivors
raise 0/764. Stage~4 further removes workload-specific candidates that
same-workload clean traces do not falsify (main-paper dual-axis funnel table).
The leave-one-database-out guard ablation ($\Sigma$FP $342\!\to\!429/551/598$
over 42 databases) is reported in the main text; this appendix adds the
per-stage funnel figure and the CE/provenance audit below.

\subsection{Mining Provenance Audit Protocol}
\label{app:provenance}

This subsection records the protocol behind the main-text \emph{mining provenance} claim (Generalizability): case-level Real-SE material does not enter the mining inputs.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Pre-registered exclusion list.} The \NumRealSE{} Real-SE case
IDs, frameworks, and (when available) fix-commit short hashes are
pre-registered in a JSON exclusion list shipped with the artifact; the list
supports the audit below and any future from-scratch re-mining study. It is
derived from Table~\ref{tab:detection-results} and is not edited after
registration.
\item \textbf{Primary library audit.} The archived mining-session logs used to build the deployed Megatron-LM verified constraint library $\mathcal{P}$ (150 \texttt{llm\_interactions\_*.log} files covering the construction window) were scanned for (i)~every fix-commit hash on the exclusion list and (ii)~any GitHub issue/PR URL of the form \texttt{github.com/.../(issues|pull)/[0-9]+}. Both scans returned zero hits: the miner messages contain framework source paths, documentation excerpts, and injection-lineage tables, not case-level bug reports.
\item \textbf{What this does and does not show.} Zero log hits rule out case-level leakage into the recorded mining transcripts. They do not rule out LLM pretraining exposure to public bug discussions---that residual channel is stated in the main-text Limitations paragraph.
\end{itemize}

\section{SQL Query Specification}
\label{app:sql}

This appendix documents the constraint-to-SQL translation rules and provides representative query examples. Alignment with the main-text formalization: $\pi_{\text{topo}}$ and $\pi_{\text{precond}}$ compile to \texttt{WHERE} filters; for relational checks, $\pi_{\text{schema}}$ is evaluated per \emph{comparison group} (the guarded tuples sharing the grouping key, e.g., all replicas of one parameter at one step) and appears as the \texttt{HAVING} predicate---matching Eq.~(violation set) and the Online Detection section of the main paper. Single-tuple checks are the singleton special case of the same shape.

\subsection{Database Schema}

\begin{sqlcode}{Database Schema}
CREATE TABLE coredump (
    step    INTEGER,    -- Training step index
    stage   TEXT,       -- Training stage identifier
    data    JSON        -- Tensor metadata as JSON
);
\end{sqlcode}

\subsubsection{DP Parameter Consistency Check}

\begin{sqlcode}{DP Parameter Consistency Check}
WITH param_stats AS (
    SELECT
        json_extract_string(data, '$.name') as param_name,
        CAST(json_extract_string(data, '$.dp') AS INTEGER) as dp_rank,
        json_extract_string(data, '$.cksum') as cksum,
        step
    FROM coredump
    WHERE stage = 'model-after-optimizer-step'
      AND json_extract_string(data, '$.cksum') IS NOT NULL
),
inconsistency_check AS (
    SELECT
        param_name, step,
        COUNT(DISTINCT cksum) as distinct_cksums,
        array_agg(DISTINCT dp_rank) as dp_ranks
    FROM param_stats
    GROUP BY param_name, step
    HAVING COUNT(DISTINCT cksum) > 1
)
SELECT * FROM inconsistency_check ORDER BY step, param_name;
\end{sqlcode}

\begin{sqlcode}{TP Weight Partition Check}

        json_extract_string(data, '$.cksum') as cksum

    WHERE stage = 'model-after-backward'
      AND json_extract_string(data, '$.name') LIKE '%qkv%'
)
SELECT param_name, COUNT(DISTINCT cksum) as unique_shards
FROM tp_params
GROUP BY param_name
HAVING COUNT(DISTINCT cksum) = 1;  -- Should be different across TP ranks
\end{sqlcode}

\section{Diagnosis Accuracy}
\label{app:diagnosis}

This section quantifies the main-text diagnosis claim ($\sim$6$\times$ candidate-set reduction) on the same 17 detected cases; it adds detail, not a new experiment. Detecting an error alone offers limited value to engineers---they also need to know \emph{which constraint was violated} and \emph{at which layer the fault manifests}. This appendix answers two questions: (i) when one of \textsc{TrainAudit}'s checks fires, does the check itself name the fault's semantic class? (ii) for complex faults that propagate across multiple layers, can the drill-down logic converge to the root cause?

\paragraph{Setup.}
\sloppypar
\textsc{TrainAudit}'s diagnosis chain consists of two stages: (1) \textit{rule names as fault signatures}---each rule's name (e.g., \texttt{optim-step-counter-monotonic}) directly encodes ``which object should satisfy which condition''; (2) \textit{C1 expander + C2 drill-down logic}---the C1 expander walks the constraint hierarchy backward along the ``upstream dependency'' template to expand a candidate source-tensor set, and the C2 drill-down logic uses runtime evidence (first-NaN step, stride offset relative to \texttt{zero\_grad}, etc.) to converge to a root cause within the expanded set. We measure this chain on the 17 Real-SE cases detected in the main paper's detection study (the boundary case triggers no rule and is excluded). For each case we record the candidate-set size $|S_{\mathrm{rule}}|$ at rule firing time, $|S_{\mathrm{C1}}|$ after the C1 expander, and $|S_{\mathrm{C2}}|$ after the C2 drill-down, taking the fix-patch-annotated true root cause ($|\text{set}|=1$) as ground truth.
\par

\paragraph{Finding 1: Rule names alone converge 53\% of the cases at the alert stage.}
Figure~\ref{fig:diagnosis-mechanism}: \textbf{9/17 (53\%)} have $|S_{\mathrm{rule}}|=1$ at alert---the rule name localizes the object (e.g., \texttt{optim-step-counter-monotonic}, \texttt{checkpoint-preserve-rng}). Naming is required at generation time. TrainCheck-style templates often report only pairwise disagreement, leaving semantic identity to the engineer.

\paragraph{Finding 2: C1+C2 compresses the search space by $\sim 6\times$ on the 47\% ambiguous cases.}
On the remaining 8/17 cases, C1 expands along upstream dependencies to a median candidate-set size of \textbf{6} (peak 12); C2 then converges with runtime evidence. Of these 8, 7 reach $|\text{set}|=1$ (5 by C1, 2 by C2); the remaining case (1/17 overall) stays ambiguous without offline signals.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.48\columnwidth]{figures/fig_diagnosis_mechanism.pdf}
\caption{C1+C2 drill-down mechanism on 17 detected Real-SE cases: 9/17 converge at rule alert, 5/17 require C1, 2/17 require C2; the median candidate-set trajectory on the 8 ambiguous cases is $1\!\to\!6\!\to\!1$.}
\Description{Chart of candidate-set sizes at rule alert, after C1 expander, and after C2 drill-down for Real-SE cases.}
